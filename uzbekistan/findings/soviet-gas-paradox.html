Gas-producing regions have the least gas access

The gas-producing regions have the lowest domestic gas access — grid access reflects Soviet political-distribution choices, not geology or income.

descriptive Real in the data, but not causally identified.

What we found

  • corr(access, distance-to-field) = +0.14 (wrong sign, p=0.64) — proximity to gas fields does not predict access.
  • The gas-producing southwest has the lowest access (Kashkadarya 26.7%, Surkhandarya 28.3%); the distant capital has the highest (Tashkent city 93.4%).
  • Producer regions average 52.7% access vs 61.3% elsewhere.

A naive distance-to-field instrument fails — usefully: the Soviets piped gas to the capital and to export, bypassing the producing rural SW. That strengthens the case that grid access is plausibly exogenous to income.

Behind it: exploration/soviet_gas_firststage.py; stat.uz environment/1243; Soviet-era Bukhara-Khiva basin field coordinates.

What this cannot carry

  • Region-level N=14 is coarse; the district file (~190 districts) gives real power once district centroids are obtained.
  • A refined instrument would be distance to the Soviet trunk-pipeline / urban distribution corridor (Bukhara→Samarkand→Jizzakh→Syrdarya→Tashkent), not fields — needs the route digitised.
