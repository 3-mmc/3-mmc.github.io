Headline

proven

The reshuffle — shortage and price hit different households

Uzbekistan ran two energy-rationing regimes in three winters, and they burdened near-disjoint sets of households — the shortage hit the off-grid periphery, the price hit the connected core.

The tariff

proven

Incidence ran on the grid, not on income

Because the gas grid is urban, a universal energy subsidy was implicitly an urban/grid/big-user subsidy — so withdrawing it is governed by infrastructure access, not income.

proven

The frozen bill — a winter-shaped price shock

The 1 May 2024 tariff raised every household's energy bill ~70%, but the extra bite lands almost entirely in winter, on grid (piped-gas) households.

proven

The winter burden hidden by annual averages

The energy burden is a winter phenomenon that annual averages wash out completely.

proven

The winter heating bite (triple-difference)

Post-reform, grid households face an extra winter payment-distress bite that the annual average hides.

proven

Reliability recovered before the tariff, not because of it

Winter 2024/25 was the most reliable winter in the panel and the most financially distressed — but the reliability recovery began a year earlier, when Russian gas imports started, before the tariff.

proven

Two winters, two crises (event study)

Grid-household payment distress spikes exactly twice — the 2022/23 supply crisis and the 2024/25 price winter — with a clean zero in between.

proven

Gains were visible, losses were masked (recomposition)

Estimating both arms in one model, the gains (ag income, better economic sentiment) show up cleanly at the household level while the losses don't appear as welfare damage — the aggregation-bias argument made structural.

proven

The eaten disinflation (CPI placebo)

The repricing is invisible in headline inflation because the tariff shock absorbed a global disinflation that every regional peer enjoyed in 2024.

proven

Revenue, not conservation — demand was inelastic

The reform's stated goal was conservation; physical energy use barely moved, so it was a pure income transfer, not an efficiency gain.

proven

The compensation channel reached 16 households

The income-targeted compensation for the tariff failed structurally — it reached almost no one — because the losers are defined by grid access, not income.

Method

revised

Robustness checks and claims we withdrew

The honesty ledger — things that looked true in a naive comparison but did not survive household fixed effects, and the claims we therefore dropped.

History

descriptive

Tsarist railway persistence in today's wealth

The 1888–1906 Trans-Caspian railway still predicts local wealth 130 years later.

descriptive

The Soviet gas paradox — "produce gas, freeze in winter"

The gas-producing regions have the lowest domestic gas access — grid access reflects Soviet political-distribution choices, not geology or income.

descriptive

The end of the gas dividend

The rent that paid for cheap gas evaporated exactly as households were asked to pay more — this is the "why now."

Agriculture

descriptive

The cotton monopsony and subsidy bunching

The 2024 ag package looks like industrial policy (clusters, per-ha subsidy, export promotion) but behaves like rent-recomposition — transfers, not productive transformation.

Banked

banked

Banked — the compulsory-college reversal cohort cliff

Uzbekistan's 1997 compulsory-vocational reform and its 2017 reversal produce one of the sharpest cohort discontinuities in survey data — possibly the only modern case of a state removing compulsory schooling years — but the outcome window hasn't matured.
