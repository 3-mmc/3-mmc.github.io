Findings · The 2024 energy tariff

Farm income gains were measurable, energy losses were not

Estimating both arms in one model, the gains (ag income, better economic sentiment) show up cleanly at the household level while the losses don't appear as welfare damage — the aggregation-bias argument made structural.

proven Identified — the estimate holds up under the panel's fixed effects.

What we found

  • Gain side is clean: ag-base households' ag-income receipt rose +3.1pp, their economic-condition rating +5.5pp.
  • Loss side does not appear as welfare damage — grid households' life satisfaction actually fell less (+0.081).
  • corr(grid, ag-base) = −0.30 — the two arms hit largely different households, so the recomposition is real, not mechanical.

What this cannot carry

  • The masked loss side is the point, not a failure — the bill shock shows up in the winter margin, not in annual welfare aggregates.
  • Whether the ag "gain" is crop revenue or subsidy transfer is settled separately (see the cotton-monopsony note).
The numbers — specification, coefficients and caveats

Claim

Estimating both arms of the recomposition in one model (post + post×grid + post×ag-base): the gain side is clean and household-level — ag-base households' ag-income receipt rose +3.1pp** and their economic-condition rating +5.5pp** — while the loss side does not appear as welfare damage (grid life satisfaction actually fell less, +0.081***). Gains visible, losses masked: the aggregation-bias argument made structural. corr(grid, ag-base) = −0.30 — the two arms hit largely different households, so the recomposition is real, not mechanical.
