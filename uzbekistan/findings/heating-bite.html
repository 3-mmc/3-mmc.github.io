Findings · The 2024 energy tariff

Grid households fell behind on winter bills after the reform

Post-reform, grid households face an extra winter payment-distress bite that the annual average hides.

proven Identified — the estimate holds up under the panel's fixed effects.

What we found

  • post × grid × winter on "couldn't pay utilities" = +1.9pp (t=2.1), on top of a general post-winter rise of +2.5pp.
  • Same triple-diff on energy share of income = +3.1pp.
  • No grid-specific winter health effect (sick / needed-medical ≈ 0), but a general post-reform winter rise in healthcare cut-backs (+7.4pp) — a coping margin, not cleanly the gas tariff.
  • Distress fell in non-winter post months and rose in winter — a seasonal heating story the annual average cancels out.

What this cannot carry

  • Identification rests on the income boom having no winter×grid signature (checked: post×grid on ln income ≈ 0).
  • Because the pre-period contains the 2022/23 supply crisis, these estimates are biased toward zero — i.e. conservative.
  • heat_require as a severity proxy is indirect; corroborate winter temperature with ERA5 if this becomes load-bearing.
The numbers — specification, coefficients and caveats

Claim

Post-reform, grid (piped-gas) households face an extra winter utility-payment bite of +1.9pp** (post×grid×winter on "couldn't pay utilities", t=2.1) beyond the general post-winter rise (+2.5pp***) — isolated from secular trends because the income boom has no winter signature. This reconciles the muted annual effect: distress fell in non-winter post months and rose in winter — a seasonal heating phenomenon the annual average washes out.

  • Because the pre-period contains the 2022/23 grid-specific supply crisis, these estimates are biased toward zero — conservative (see 05 Two winters, two crises — the event study).

Backing code

Origin: exploration/tariff_recomp_triple.py (N1 block); canonical: dissertation/build_results.py T4.

# N1 — WINTER TRIPLE-DIFF (post×grid×winter = extra heating-season bite); HH+calmonth FE
d['winter'] = d.month.isin([12, 1, 2, 3]).astype(float)   # heating season
d['p_w'] = d.post*d.winter; d['g_w'] = d.grid*d.winter
d['p_g'] = d.post*d.grid;   d['p_g_w'] = d.post*d.grid*d.winter
X3 = ['post', 'p_g', 'p_w', 'g_w', 'p_g_w']
for y in ['util_distress', 'reduced_food', 'fies', 'reduced_hc']:
    rows += fereg(d, y, X3)        # HH FE + calendar-month FE, cluster HH

Caveats

  • Identification rests on the income boom having no winter×grid signature — checked: post×grid on ln(income) ≈ 0 (15 Robustness and withdrawn claims).
  • heat_require as severity proxy is indirect; corroborate winter 2024/25 temperature with ERA5 if the seasonal claim becomes load-bearing.
