Findings · The 2024 energy tariff

Annual averages hide the winter cost increase

The energy burden is a winter phenomenon that annual averages wash out completely.

proven Identified — the estimate holds up under the panel's fixed effects.

What we found

  • Grid households' median energy share of cash income is flat across the reform on an all-months basis (5.1% → 5.0%) but rises 6.2% → 8.0% in winter.
  • Triple-diff (post × grid × winter) on the income share = +3.1pp.
  • Nominal income grew ~+20%/yr for both groups — enough to cover the average bill but not the winter spike.

The numbers — specification, coefficients and caveats

Claim

The burden is a winter phenomenon that annual averages wash out. Grid households' median energy share of cash income is flat across the reform on an all-months basis (5.1% → 5.0%) but rises 6.2% → 8.0% in winter; the triple-diff (post×grid×winter) on the share is +3.1pp*. Nominal income growth (~+20%/yr, both groups) covered the average bill but not the winter spike — masking has a temporal dimension alongside the cross-sectional and macro ones.

Backing code

dissertation/build_results.py (income & share construction; T1/T4 blocks):

# cash income & burden
for stub in ['wage', 'pension', 'benefit', 'aginc', 'selfempinc', 'otherinc']:
    d['inc_' + stub] = d[stub + '_amount'].where(is_yes(d[stub + '_past30d']) == 1, 0).fillna(0)
d['income_total'] = d[[c for c in d.columns if c.startswith('inc_')]].sum(axis=1)
d['energy_share'] = np.where(d.income_total > 0, d.pay_total / d.income_total, np.nan)
d.loc[d.energy_share > 1, 'energy_share'] = np.nan   # trim absurd shares

# T4 bottom panel: the share triple-diff
rows += fereg(d, 'energy_share', ['post', 'p_g', 'p_w', 'g_w', 'p_g_w'])

First run: exploration/claude_newangles.py §G.

Caveats

  • Denominator is cash income only (wage/pension/benefit/ag/self-emp/other amounts; excludes in-kind and the remittance module). Shares >100% trimmed. Treat the levels as descriptive and the triple-diff as the estimate.
