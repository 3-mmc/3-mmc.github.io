Findings · The 2024 energy tariff

Shortages and price rises hit different households

Uzbekistan ran two energy-rationing regimes in three winters, and they burdened near-disjoint sets of households — the shortage hit the off-grid periphery, the price hit the connected core.

proven Identified — the estimate holds up under the panel's fixed effects.

What this cannot carry

  • Different currencies: service loss (self-reported hours) vs payment distress (couldn't-pay) are measured differently — inherent to comparing a ration with a price, not a fixable defect.
  • Cross-sectional correlation of two household-level exposures, not a within-household FE estimate (there's one rationing burden and one pricing burden per household).
  • Attrition: 1,175 of ~1,500 crisis-winter households are present in the price winter; the quartile pattern isn't driven by selective exit.
  • External corroboration: exports to China were "100% halted" in Dec 2022 to cover domestic demand (Statistics Agency / Uztransgaz).
The numbers — specification, coefficients and caveats

Claim

Uzbekistan ran two energy-allocation regimes in three winters, and they burdened near-disjoint sets of households. Rationing-by-shortage (winter 2022/23) fell on the periphery — households off the piped grid, cut to 15 hours of power a day. Rationing-by-price (the 2024/25 tariff) fell on the connected core — the same never-out, mostly-grid households that the outages had spared. The cross-regime correlation is negative: the winter's worst-rationed quartile had the lowest later payment distress. The state did not shift the burden down or up the distribution — it swapped who carried it. This is the headline; the grid-incidence results (09 Incidence ran on the grid, not income) are its mechanism — the price burden ran along the grid because the grid is where the subsidy always lived.

Why this is the headline

Barzel (1974): a queue is a price, paid in a different currency by different people. Weitzman (1977): prices vs rationing as rival ways to move a good to those who need it — and this panel answers it within one country, across the switch. The China gas reversal (12 The end of the gas dividend) is what forced the switch — a queue conserves nothing once the marginal molecule is purchased in hard currency — so it belongs in Ch.2 (mechanism) and Ch.6 (discussion), not in the RQ.

Caveats

  • Different currencies. The two burdens are measured differently — service loss (self-reported

hours yesterday, averaged over ~4 rounds) vs payment distress (couldn't-pay-utilities). This is inherent to comparing an in-kind ration with a price, not a fixable defect. Own it in one paragraph; do not apologise for it.

  • Not contractual seniority. The periphery-first ordering is revealed preference, not a claim

about contract terms (which are not public). Now corroborated externally: exports to China were "100% halted" in Dec 2022 to cover domestic demand (Statistics Agency / Uztransgaz) — the queue's international position (T10/F7, dissertation/build_trade_mechanism.py). See Queues, prices, and the China margin §2.

  • The reshuffle is a cross-sectional correlation of two household-level exposures; it is not a

within-household FE estimate (there is one rationing burden and one pricing burden per household). The FE machinery lives in the mechanism results (03 The heating bite — winter triple-diff).

  • Attrition: 1,175 of ~1,500 crisis-winter households are also present in the price winter; the

quartile pattern is not driven by selective exit (grid share and distress move monotonically).
