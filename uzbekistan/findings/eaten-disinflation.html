Findings · The tariff

The eaten disinflation (CPI placebo)

The repricing is invisible in headline inflation because the tariff shock absorbed a global disinflation that every regional peer enjoyed in 2024.

proven Identified — the estimate holds up under the panel's fixed effects.

What we found — three masks

  1. Seasonal: annual energy shares flat while winter shares jump.
  2. Cross-sectional: the non-grid half never had the subsidy, so it doesn't move.
  3. Macro: in 2024 every regional peer disinflated sharply while Uzbekistan stayed flat.

  • Against a regional counterfactual of ~−5pp, "headline stable" is not benign — the tariff shock ate ~5pp of disinflation.
  • Within-survey micro check: households' own reported bread prices were +0% in 2024 (after +56% in 2022, +20% in 2023), while electricity payments rose +60% — food relief and energy shock in the same survey, same month.
  • Mechanism (WB deck reconstruction): services CPI +28% YoY post-reform, headline ~flat because food CPI collapsed to ~2.5%.

Behind it: datasets/external_worldbank/central_asia_panel_long.csv; dissertation/build_results.py (F4); analysis/09_cpi_chart_reconstruction.py.

What this cannot carry

  • The cross-country comparison is a placebo/benchmark, not an identified DiD.
  • Bread-price jump in 2025 (+64%) is unexplained — don't lean on 2025 food prices.
  • Food disinflation is global prices + input-cost subsidies, not an import glut (food exports +22% rule out a glut).
The numbers — specification, coefficients and caveats

Claim

The repricing is invisible in aggregates because of three masks: (i) seasonal aggregation — annual energy shares flat while winter shares jump; (ii) cross-sectional aggregation — the non-grid half never had the subsidy; (iii) macro aggregation — in 2024 every regional peer disinflated sharply (KAZ 14.5→8.7, KGZ 10.8→5.0, AZE 8.8→2.2, MDA 13.4→4.7, MNG 10.3→6.2) while Uzbekistan stayed flat (10.0→9.6). Against the regional counterfactual (~−5pp), "headline stable" is not benign: the tariff shock absorbed a global disinflation that would otherwise have delivered ~5% inflation.

Evidence

  • Cross-country placebo (N7): WDI headline inflation, 2024 vs 2023 — UZB the only non-disinflating peer.
  • Within-survey micro-validation: in households' own reported prices (L2CU medians), bread +0% in 2024 (after +56% in 2022, +20% in 2023), flour falling — while electricity payments +60%. Food relief and energy shock experienced simultaneously, in the same survey.
  • Mechanism (WB deck reconstruction, analysis/09_cpi_chart_reconstruction.py): services CPI +28% YoY post-reform, headline ~flat (~9.7%) because food CPI collapsed to ~2.5%.
  • Attribution caution: food disinflation is global prices + input-cost subsidies, not domestic supply or an import glut (food imports +5.7%, food exports +22% — rising exports rule out a glut).

Backing code

dissertation/build_results.py F4:

ca = pd.read_csv(ROOT / "datasets/external_worldbank/central_asia_panel_long.csv")
inf = ca[(ca.indicator == 'inflation') & (ca.year.between(2019, 2024))]
piv = inf.pivot_table(index='year', columns='country', values='value')
peers = ['Kazakhstan', 'Kyrgyz Republic', 'Azerbaijan', 'Georgia', 'Armenia', 'Mongolia', 'Moldova']
ax.plot(piv.index, piv[peers].median(axis=1), color='grey', lw=2, ls='--', label='Peer median')
ax.plot(piv.index, piv['Uzbekistan'], color='crimson', lw=2.5, label='Uzbekistan')

Caveats

  • Bread-price jump in 2025 (+64%) is unexplained (possible liberalization/composition) — don't lean on 2025 food prices without checking.
  • The cross-country comparison is a placebo/benchmark, not an identified DiD.
