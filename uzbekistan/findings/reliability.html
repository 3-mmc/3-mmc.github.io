Findings · The 2024 energy tariff

Electricity supply improved before the tariff, not because of it

Winter 2024/25 was the most reliable winter in the panel and the most financially distressed — but the reliability recovery began a year earlier, when Russian gas imports started, before the tariff.

proven Identified — the estimate holds up under the panel's fixed effects.

What we found

  • Winter 2024/25: 23.6 electricity hrs/day, 1.6% heat disruption — the best in the panel — despite the highest heating need (88% needing heat). Also the most distressed: 9.0% couldn't pay.
  • The recovery started in winter 2023/24, right after Russian gas imports began (Oct 2023) — before the May 2024 tariff.
  • FE estimates: electricity hours post × winter +1.02 hrs/day; heat disruption post −16.5pp; gas disruption (grid HH) post × winter −2.6pp.

The honest phrasing: "imports restored reliability; the tariff made households pay for it." Never "the tariff bought reliability."

What this cannot carry

  • Reliability could also reflect ongoing grid investment, not only imports — another reason to avoid the causal "tariff bought it" claim.
  • High heating need rules out a mild-winter artifact.
The numbers — specification, coefficients and caveats

Claim

Winter 2024/25 was the most reliable winter in the panel (23.6 elec hrs/day, 1.6% heat disruption) despite the highest heating need (88% heat_require) — and simultaneously the most financially distressed (9.0% couldn't pay). But the recovery began in winter 2023/24, BEFORE the tariff, right after Russian gas imports started (Oct 2023). Claim exactly: "imports restored reliability; the tariff made households pay for it" — never "the tariff bought reliability."

This is the narrative hinge of "From Shortage to Price": it pre-empts the examiner objection "but the lights stayed on — wasn't it worth it?"

Backing code

Outcome construction and by-winter table, exploration/claude_newangles.py §C/§E (canonical FE: build_results.py T5):

# L2CU service-quality variables nobody used
d['elec_hours'] = d.electricity_yesterday          # hours, 0-24
d['gas_disrupt'] = yes_no_nan(d.gas_disruption)
d['heat_disrupt'] = yes_no_nan(d.heat_disruption)  # asked only if heat_require == Yes

# by-winter means (winter labelled by Jan year)
w = d[d.winter == 1].copy()
w['wy'] = np.where(w.month == 12, w.year + 1, w.year)
ts = w.groupby('wy').agg(elec_hours=('elec_hours', 'mean'),
                         gas_disrupt=('gas_disrupt', 'mean'),
                         heat_disrupt=('heat_disrupt', 'mean'),
                         util_distress=('util_distress', 'mean'))

# T5 — reliability FE
rows = (fereg(d, 'elec_hours', ['post', 'p_w'])
        + fereg(d[d.grid == 1], 'gas_disrupt', ['post', 'p_w'])
        + fereg(d, 'heat_disrupt', ['post', 'p_g']))

Caveats

  • Reliability could also reflect ongoing grid investment, not only imports — another reason to avoid the causal "tariff bought it" claim.
  • High heat_require rules out a mild-winter artifact; if anything the bill shock landed in a high-need winter.
