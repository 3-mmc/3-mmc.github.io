Findings · Agriculture

Cotton prices fell while almost every other crop rose

The 2024 ag package looks like industrial policy (clusters, per-ha subsidy, export promotion) but behaves like rent-recomposition — transfers, not productive transformation.

descriptive Real in the data, but not causally identified.

What we found

  1. Cotton monopsony: of 52 FAO commodities, 43 rose (median +55%) over 2022–24, while the two biggest falls are both cotton — raw seed-cotton −56%, then frozen at 4,500,000 sum/tonne for 24 straight months (a set price, not a cleared one).
  2. Subsidy bunching: ag-household "gains" are transfer-driven, not crop revenue — reports at exactly 1,000,000 sum (the per-hectare subsidy) rose from ~14% to 24% of ag-income reports, while ag-base wage receipt fell −2.5pp.
  3. No productivity signal: the rural boom was fiscal, not physical — NDVI ≈ normal, official output ≈ trend, ag emissions flat, value +8.5% > volume ~+5%.

Income decomposition (post × ag-base on receipt): aginc +3.1pp, wage −2.5pp, other −2.1pp. National transfer share of income 27.1 → 29.6% (2023–25) while production share fell.

Behind it: exploration/ag_gains.py; datasets/producer-prices_uzb.csv (FAO); ClimateTrace ag emissions. Confirms Babadjanov & Petrick's monopsony qualitatively, with numbers they lacked.

What this cannot carry

  • World cotton softened ~20–35% over 2022–24, so part is global — but the Uzbek raw-cotton fall is steeper and runs opposite to the +55% domestic median.
  • Don't claim orchestration intent, or that energy savings financed agriculture (the ag package came first and was partly externally funded).
The numbers — specification, coefficients and caveats

Scope rule: this is labelled descriptive context (SQ4), not a second identified leg.

Claim

The 2024 ag package has the form of industrial policy (clusters, per-ha subsidy, export promotion) but the substance of rent-recomposition: (1) of 52 FAO commodities, 43 rose (median +55%) 2022–24 while the two biggest falls are both cotton — raw seed-cotton −56% (then frozen at 4,500,000 sum/tonne for 24 straight months — a set price, not a cleared one); (2) ag-household "gains" are transfer-driven, not crop revenue — reports at exactly 1,000,000 sum (the per-hectare subsidy) rose from ~14% to 24% of ag-income reports, while ag-base wage receipt fell −2.5pp***; (3) no productivity signal — the rural boom was fiscal, not physical (NDVI ≈ normal, official output ≈ trend, ag emissions flat, value +8.5% > volume ~+5%).

Confirms Babadjanov & Petrick's monopsony qualitatively — with numbers they lacked.

Evidence

  • Chart: exploration/outputs/charts/02_cotton_monopsony.png
  • Income decomposition (post×agbase on receipt): aginc +3.1pp*, wage −2.5pp*, other −2.1pp***.
  • National transfer share of income 27.1→29.6% (2023–25) while production share fell.
  • 4-way fiscal-not-physical check: NDVI −0.3%; stat.uz output ~+5% (trend); ClimateTrace ag emissions +0.3%; value>volume wedge.

Backing code

exploration/ag_gains.py — the bunching test and the monopsony price comparison:

print("IS THE AG-INCOME GAIN CROP REVENUE OR THE 1M-SUM/HA SUBSIDY? (bunching)")
a = d[d.aginc == 1].copy(); a['amt'] = pd.to_numeric(a.aginc_amount, errors='coerce')
a['exact1m'] = (a.amt == 1_000_000).astype(float)
for y in sorted(a.yr.dropna().unique()):
    s = a[a.yr == y]
    print(f"{int(y):<6d}{len(s):>16d}{s.amt.median():>14,.0f}{s.exact1m.mean()*100:>19.0f}%")

print("MONOPSONY TEST — did farm-gate PRODUCER PRICES rise? (FAO, LCU/tonne, annual)")
fao = pd.read_csv("datasets/producer-prices_uzb.csv")
fao = fao[fao['Months'] == 'Annual value']
piv = fao.pivot_table(index='Item', columns='Year', values='Value', aggfunc='first')

Caveats

  • World cotton softened ~20–35% over 2022–24, so part is global — but the Uzbek raw-cotton fall is steeper and runs opposite to the +55% domestic median; domestic monopsony amplifies it.
  • Don't claim orchestration intent or that energy savings financed agriculture (fungibility + reverse timing: ag package came first, partly externally funded).
