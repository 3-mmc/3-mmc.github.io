Findings · The 2024 energy tariff

Who paid depended on gas connection, not income

Because the gas grid is urban, a universal energy subsidy was implicitly an urban/grid/big-user subsidy — so withdrawing it is governed by infrastructure access, not income.

proven Identified — the estimate holds up under the panel's fixed effects.

What we found

  • Pre-reform median "subsidised" bill: piped 202,500 vs bottled/other 75,000 UZS (2.7×); urban 150k vs rural 112.5k.
  • Top-quartile pre-reform users: 88.5% piped, ~50% urban (vs 39% / 34% for the rest).
  • Administrative grid gradient (stat.uz district-level % apartments with piped gas): cities ~96–100%, rural districts 15–35%, national ~56% — administrative proof that tariff exposure is urban.
  • Core DiD: "tariff too high" +0.330 (t=37); couldn't pay utilities +0.030 (t=11); reduced food +0.015 (t=7); FIES and dirty-fuel switching did not rise.
  • Welfare stress rose more among urban households — this inverts the original proposal's prior that the rural poor would be hit.

The point: the development question "do block tariffs protect the poor?" largely misfires here — the rural poor were never in the subsidised system.

Behind it: incidence pipeline analysis/01–08 (two-way FE DiD in l2cu_common.py); stat.uz environment/1243.

What this cannot carry

  • The naive pre/post absorbs seasonality but not secular 2024 trends — the identified claims live in the exposure interactions and the winter triple-diff, not the raw pre/post.
  • The 2.7× bill ratio is the subsidy's size gradient, not yet an estimate of harm.
The numbers — specification, coefficients and caveats

Claim

In a country where the gas grid is urban, a universal energy subsidy is implicitly an urban / grid / big-user subsidy — so withdrawing it is mechanically a grid-incidence event governed by infrastructure access, not income. The development question "do block tariffs protect the poor?" largely misfires: the rural poor were never in the subsidised system.

Evidence

  • Pre-reform median "subsidised" bill: piped 202,500 vs bottled/other 75,000 UZS (2.7×); urban 150k vs rural 112.5k.
  • Top-quartile pre-reform users: 88.5% piped, ~50% urban (vs 39% / 34% for the rest).
  • Administrative grid gradient (stat.uz environment/1243, district-level % apartments with piped gas): cities ~96–100%, rural districts 15–35%, national ~56% — administrative proof that tariff exposure is urban.
  • Core DiD (HH + calendar-month FE): "tariff too high" +0.330 (t=37); couldn't pay utilities +0.030 (t=11); reduced food +0.015 (t=7); FIES and dirty-fuel switching did not rise.
  • Welfare stress rose more among urban households (validated ag-based rural proxy) — inverts the original proposal's prior.
