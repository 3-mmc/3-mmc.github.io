The research behind this atlas

These datasets were assembled for a dissertation on the 1 May 2024 household energy-tariff reform — "From Shortage to Price": the state converted a physical winter energy crisis (2022/23 outages) into a financial one (2024/25 winter bills), and annual averages, headline CPI and income-targeted compensation are all blind to the conversion. The argument in five acts

Act 0 — The subsidy of shortage

The Soviet-built grid delivered gas as a cheap implicit transfer to the connected (median bills frozen in nominal terms 2021–23). But the rent financing it collapsed (production −37%, net importer 2023) and the system began rationing by outage: winter 2022/23 — 20.8 hrs of electricity/day, gas and heat disruptions. A low price for energy you couldn't get (Kornai).

Act 1 — Imports fix the quantity

Russian gas imports (Oct 2023) restored winter reliability a season before the tariff: winter 2023/24 was already near-normal.

Act 2 — The tariff reprices the fix

1 May 2024: cost-recovery tariffs pass the import bill to the grid-connected. Bills +70% generally; grid winter bills double; winter energy share 6.2→8.0%; winter distress +3pp. Rationing by outage became rationing by bill.

Act 3 — Three masks

The repricing is invisible in aggregates: (i) seasonal aggregation (annual shares flat), (ii) cross-sectional aggregation (the non-grid half never had the subsidy), (iii) macro aggregation (food disinflation absorbed the services shock — UZB CPI flat at ~10% while every peer disinflated ~5pp).

Act 4 — The recomposition

The fiscal space moved to the production side — per-hectare transfers, export promotion, cluster credits — whose gains are visible, untargeted and partly captured (cotton monopsony; subsidy bunching). Compensation through the consumer channel reached ~16 households.

Verdict

In principle the reform swapped a deadweight loss (shortage) for a transfer (bills). In practice: incidence ran on Soviet-era infrastructure access, not income; the losers' compensation channel failed; the proceeds were recomposed into captured production subsidies; and conservation — the stated goal — didn't happen. The orthodoxy evaluates the removal; the welfare and political economy live in the redeployment.
