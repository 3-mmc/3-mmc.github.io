About

What this is

An atlas of Uzbek economic, energy and household data, and the research findings drawn from it. The data came together for a dissertation on the 1 May 2024 household energy-tariff reform; the atlas outgrew that question and is now source-agnostic. You can read the findings, browse a topic, or ignore both and go straight to the raw series.

Everything here is static. There is no server, no account and no tracking — the pages fetch a set of JSON files and draw them in your browser.

How to read a finding

Every finding carries a label saying how much weight it can bear. This matters more than it sounds: a correlation in this data and an identified causal estimate look identical on a chart, and are worth completely different things.

  • proven — identified. The estimate survives household fixed effects, and the design rules out the obvious confounds.
  • descriptive — real in the data, but not causally identified. Often the most interesting material; treat it as a pattern, not a mechanism.
  • revised — the honesty ledger. Claims that looked true in a naive comparison and did not survive a harder test.
  • banked — real, but the outcome window has not matured. Parked until the data exists.
  • null — tried and killed. Recorded so the same dead end is not re-hunted.

Each finding page leads with the claim in plain language and a chart. The specification, the coefficients and the caveats sit behind a fold marked The numbers, so you can take it as far as you want.

Where the data comes from

Household microdata

Listening to the Citizens of Uzbekistan (L2CU) — a monthly household panel, roughly 2,500 households across 82 rounds from September 2018 to June 2025. The analytical sample is the 1,862 households whose pre-reform gas connection status is known. Everything about bills, payment distress, electricity hours and migration comes from here.

MICS6 (2021–22) — 15,600 households and 71,600 people, used for the pre-reform fuel and wealth baseline and the education cohort work.

Official statistics

stat.uz, the Statistics Agency of Uzbekistan — 657 files across eight topics, assembled into a region-by-year panel of 266 indicators. This is the source for regional energy, agriculture, labour, health and the district-level gas access gradient.

International sources

  • World Bank WDI — 1,583 indicator series for Uzbekistan, 1960 to 2025, plus a 15-country comparator panel for the inflation benchmark.
  • FAO — monthly consumer price indices and annual producer prices by commodity.
  • UN Comtrade — commodity trade, used for the gold export share.
  • WFP seasonal explorer — ten-day rainfall and NDVI composites for 14 regions and 199 districts.
  • Meta Data for Good — the Relative Wealth Index, 21,382 grid cells from a 2021 snapshot.
  • Energy Institute and Ember, via Our World in Data — long-run gas and electricity series; Maddison Project for pre-1990 output.

Things worth knowing before you use a number

  • Household energy payments are expenditures — price times quantity, often lagged about a month and heaped at round numbers. There is no kilowatt-hour denominator anywhere in this data, which is why no demand elasticity is estimated at household level.
  • The energy burden denominator is cash income only. It excludes in-kind income and the remittance module, so treat burden levels as descriptive and the difference-in-differences as the estimate.
  • The Relative Wealth Index is a single 2021 snapshot, so in the correlate tool it pairs with whichever years you pick on the other axis.
  • Cross-country inflation comparisons here are a benchmark, not an identified difference-in-differences.
  • Correlations in the explore tools carry no controls and no fixed effects. They are for looking, not for concluding.

Colour and accessibility

The chart palette is validated for protanopia and deuteranopia rather than chosen by eye, and both light and dark modes were stepped and checked separately against the surfaces they actually render on. Every chart has a table view, so no value is reachable only by hovering, and no value is carried by colour alone. If something here is still hard to read, that is a bug worth reporting.

Building it

Two scripts, both standard-library Python: dashboard/build_data.py reads the datasets and writes the JSON payloads; dashboard/build_site.py assembles the pages and generates the finding pages from the research notes. bash dashboard/publish.sh runs both and pushes.
